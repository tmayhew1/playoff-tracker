Value Added (VA)

A points-equivalent valuation of every box-score contribution, stated in standard mathematical notation.

Version 2 (playoff-tracker) — supersedes the v1 definition shipped in nba-projects; divergences itemized in §8. Authoritative implementations: app/scoring.js (client) and scripts/R/scrape_common.R (bake), which agree to the decimal.

Value Added answers one question in one unit: how many points did this player produce, above or below what the league would have produced in the same workload?

1Notation

1.1Player observation

A stat line — a game, a playoff run, or a season — is the vector

x = ( MP, G, PTS, AST, STL, BLK, TOV, DRB, ORB, FGM, FGA, 3PM, 3PA, FTM, FTA )

with two-point shooting derived rather than sourced:

2PM = FGM − 3PM 2PA = FGA − 3PA

VA(x) ≡ 0 when MP ≤ 0.

1.2League baselines

For each season y, a baseline vector λ(y) is computed once from that season’s player season-totals table and stored in app/data/league-averages.json. Two families of quantity, computed two different ways on purpose (§3).

Per-minute rate baselines — minutes-weighted medians (§2):

μPTS, μAST, μSTL, μBLK, μTOV, μDRB, μORB

Conversion constants — league aggregate ratios over all players in season y:

p3 = Σ3PMΣ3PA p2 = Σ2PMΣ2PA pF = ΣFTMΣFTA pG = ΣFGMΣFGA
Π = ΣFGA − ΣORB + ΣTOV + 0.475 ΣFTA possessions — Hollinger estimator
π = ΣPTSΠ κ = ΣPTSΣFGM points per possession · points per made field goal
ρD = ΣDRBΣTRB ρO = ΣORBΣTRB = 1 − ρD ν = 5ΠΣMP rebound shares · possessions per on-court minute ( = pace ⁄ 48 )
Λ = 5ΣTRBΣMP rebound opportunities per on-court minute, at one end

Λ is used only by the rebound credit (§4.3), ν only by the defensive extension (§6). Because ΣMP counts player minutes — five per team-minute — the factor of 5 converts to possessions per team-minute.

2The minutes-weighted median

For N players in season y with stat totals Xi and minutes mi > 0, let ri = Xi ⁄ mi and let (·) denote the ordering r(1) ≤ r(2) ≤ … ≤ r(N). Then

μX = r(k*) k* = min { k : Σj ≤ k m(j) ≥ 12 Σj mj }

In words: the rate of the median league minute. Half of all NBA minutes are played above μX, half below.

Why not the two obvious alternatives. The minutes-weighted mean (the league aggregate rate) is pulled upward by a handful of high-usage stars, so “average” ends up describing nobody. The unweighted per-player median lets a 40-minute call-up outvote a 2,800-minute starter. Weighting the median by minutes fixes both: the baseline is the typical minute of basketball, which is exactly the thing VA charges a player for consuming.

3Why baselines are computed two ways

The per-minute baselines define “typical” — the counterfactual a player is measured against — so they must be robust to skew, hence the weighted median.

The conversion constants (π, κ, ρD, ρO, p3, p2, pF, pG) do not define “typical”; they translate an event into points. A possession is worth the league’s actual points-per-possession, not the median player’s. These stay aggregate ratios.

4The master equation

Every one of the ten categories has the same shape:

VA(x, λ) = Σc ∈ C wc (rc − λc) nc ( player rate − league rate ) × opportunity × price in points

where C is the ten categories and each term supplies its own rate rc, baseline λc, opportunity nc, and price wc:

where the rebound credit γ is per-player, not a constant — it is the one price that depends on the player’s own rate as well as the league’s. See §4.3.

4.2Reading the weights

  • Assist. An assist is worth the field goal it created, κ, times the share of those baskets that would not have happened without the pass, (1 − pG). Deliberately opinionated: it refuses to credit the passer for the league-baseline conversion the shooter would have managed anyway.
  • Steal vs. block. A steal ends the possession outright, so it earns a full π. A block only ends it if the defense secures the carom — about ρD of the time — so it earns πρD. The same asymmetry is reused verbatim in the defensive extension (§6.3).
  • Rebound cross-weighting. A rebound is credited a possession discounted by the probability the team would not have gotten it anyway. Defensive boards are the common outcome, so they scale by the smaller ρO; offensive boards are rarer and scale by the larger ρD. γ = 5⁄4 is not a fitted constant — it is the “one of five claimants” correction derived in §4.3.
  • Volume and efficiency are separate terms, not a net. A high-usage scorer at league efficiency has done something real (absorbed difficulty); a low-usage scorer at elite efficiency has also done something real. Netting them erases both. VA pays for each.

4.3The rebound credit γ

γ answers a question the other nine terms never have to ask: a rebound is the one box-score event that is guaranteed to be allocated and rivalrous. Every miss produces exactly one rebound, and exactly one of ten players on the floor gets it. So “would this have happened anyway?” has a real answer.

The contest. Under a Luce / Bradley–Terry model, give each of the five defenders a claim weight d and each of the five offensive players a claim weight o. Then P(defense secures) = 5d/(5d+5o) = ρD, and the team’s securing odds are Ω = ρD/ρO = d/o. Remove one of the five defenders and Ω′ = 4d/5o = (4/5)Ω, so the odds of losing the possession rise by exactly 5⁄4 — independent of ρ, of era, and of which team is rebounding.

But VA multiplies a probability, not an odds, and the two differ. Working in probability space, and generalizing off the “exactly one fifth” assumption to a player who claims a fraction q of the boards available at his end:

γ = 11 − q q = REB ⁄ MPΛ Λ = laREBoppPerM, 0.9071 in 2025-26

Every miss is rebounded at one end or the other, so a player on the floor sees Λ chances per minute at his own end. The construction is self-checking: d/(d+o) recomputed from these same sums returns ρD exactly.

ρ cancels out of the correction. Substituting q = sρ (where s is the player’s share of his team’s boards) into 1/(1 − sρ) gives the same expression with no ρ in it. ρ still prices the board — πρO for a defensive rebound, πρD for an offensive one — but it plays no part in the credit. One formula covers both ends.

It needs no team data. Λ is a league quantity, so q comes straight off the player’s own line. This matters: every other VA term reads only the player’s stat line and league baselines, which is what makes a 1987 season comparable to a 2026 one. A team-relative γ would break that — measured on 2025-26, players inside a narrow DRB/min band saw 5–7% γ swings from teammates alone, so identical production would have scored differently on different rosters. It doesn’t here.

Special cases. The league-average rebounder has q = 0.2ρ, giving

γ = 11 − 0.2ρ = 55 − ρ ⟹ γDRB ≈ 1.174 γORB ≈ 1.053

— the “one of five” constant, and the fallback for any season baked before Λ existed. The odds-space 5⁄4 = 1.25 that VA shipped through July 2026 is the ρ → 1 limit of that expression: correct as an odds ratio, but used as though it were a probability, which overstated defensive credit ~6% and offensive credit ~19%. At the top of the distribution the per-player form runs the other way — a player covering a third of his end reaches γ ≈ 1.5.

Estimation. q is unshrunk by design, matching every other VA category (none of which is shrunk). A season is 1,400–2,300 opportunities, putting binomial error on γ under 1.5%; a single game is ~32 and swings hard, exactly as one night’s shooting does in the efficiency terms. q is clamped to [0, 0.5] so γ ≤ 2 stays finite on 1-minute garbage-time lines — the only rows where the clamp ever binds.

4.4What γ assumes

Three assumptions remain, each a place the model could still be wrong.

  1. Independence of irrelevant alternatives. Removing a claimant redistributes his share proportionally across the other nine. Real rebounding is closer to matched pairs — the opposing big absorbs most of a removed center’s share, not the floor uniformly.
  2. A league-uniform opportunity rate. Λ is a league constant, but the boards available at a given player’s end depend on his team’s pace and his opponents’ shooting. Using the team’s own rate instead moves γ by a median of 0.74% (p90 2.11%, r = 0.985) — less than the binomial noise already in q, and it would cost the context-freedom above. Deliberately traded away.
  3. The marginal board is an average board. q counts an uncontested carom the same as a won contest, and most defensive rebounds are uncontested. The boards a strong rebounder adds are drawn from the contested tail, where recovery risk is far above ρO — which argues every γ here is understated for players who actually box out. This is the one objection no version of the model addresses; closing it needs contested-rebound data the box score does not carry.
Two open items. μDRB (minutes-weighted median) sits ~11% below the league aggregate rate because rebounding is right-skewed and positionally concentrated, so the median baseline already tilts this term toward bigs and γ widens the same spread. Separately, the league earns roughly 1.8× more positive VA from offensive rebounds than defensive ones — two similarly-sized populations of over-performers paid at ρD/ρO ≈ 2.85 different rates. That is a baseline question, not a γ question, and it remains open.

5Exact decomposition

The category vector is the summand of the master equation:

VAc(x, λ) = wc (rc − λc) nc Σc ∈ C VAc = VA

This identity is a product requirement, not a happy accident: every UI surface — leaderboard total, compare-panel header, stacked category bars, percentile context, comp-shape vectors — is computed from the same ten terms, so the parts always reconcile to the headline to the decimal.

The per-game category vector used downstream (§7) is

v(x) = ( VAc(x, λ)G )c ∈ C ∈ ℝ10

6VA+ — the defensive extension

Box-score defense is two events wide. VA+ adds a fifth category built from points actually allowed.

6.1Bayesian rating

Each player-season carries two defensive ratings: Dest, the box-score estimate, and Dpbp, points allowed per 100 possessions while on the floor (play-by-play, 2000-01+). With defended possessions Πp = MP · ν and prior strength Π0,

θ = ΠpΠp + Π0 D = θ Dpbp + (1 − θ) Dest Π0 = 1500regular season 500playoffs

The estimate is an informed prior worth Π0 possessions of evidence; real possessions overtake it as they accrue. A full-time starter lands ~75% on play-by-play; a 300-minute season stays mostly prior — which is what stops a small-sample on-court fluke from topping the leaderboard. The lighter playoff prior reflects both the smaller samples available and the noisier estimate behind them. Seasons with no play-by-play source are simply all-prior (θ = 0).

The team baseline T blends with the same θ, so the individual term below subtracts like from like.

6.2Net rating

With league line L = 100π and team edge E = L − T:

net = ( T − D ) + w E IND: edge over his own team  +  TM⁺: share of the team’s edge vs. league

6.3The earned share

Splitting team defense five ways equally credits a passenger like an anchor. The share is instead earned, with stocks priced exactly as VA prices them — a block is worth ρD of a steal:

s = STL + ρD BLKMP we = clamp ( 0.2 ssteam , 0.05 , 1 )
w = we E ≥ 0 — credit is earned clamp ( 0.4 − we , 0.05 , 1 ) E < 0 — blame shrinks with activity

The bad-defense branch is the earned share mirrored around the 1-in-5 split: contesting shields you from a collective failure, passivity draws more of it. Both branches conserve the team pot up to the clamps and are continuous at E = 0. Multi-team rows fall back to the plain vs-league form (w = 1).

6.4Defensive value

dVA = net100 · ν · MP VA+ = VA + dVA

VA+ is undefined — and hidden — when neither rating source exists.

7Derived instruments

7.1Shot-zone value (1996-97+)

For zone z ∈ { 0-3 ft, 3-10 ft, 10-16 ft, 16 ft-3PT } with league zone accuracy pz — the 2-point term of §4, parameterized by distance:

VAz = 2 ( MzAz − pz ) Az

Held deliberately outside C: shot-location data begins in 1996-97, and folding it into the core vector would punch holes in every earlier season’s decomposition and career totals.

7.2Closest comps

For player-seasons a, b with vectors v(a), v(b) (§5):

cos(a,b) = ⟨ v(a) , v(b) ⟩‖v(a)‖ ‖v(b)‖ mag(a,b) = min ( ‖v(a)‖ , ‖v(b)‖ )max ( ‖v(a)‖ , ‖v(b)‖ )
S(a,b) = cos(a,b) × mag(a,b) archetype match × level match

Admissible only under | MPGa − MPGb | ≤ 7 (same minutes role), G ≥ 8, and cos ≥ 0.3 — a clearly different archetype is never a comp.

The Shooting lens applies the identical cos × mag form to the 6-dimensional shooting profile (4 zones + 3-Pointers + Free Throws), additionally gated on shot diet: writing δ(x) = 3PA ⁄ FGA for the share of a player’s field-goal attempts taken from three, a comp is admissible only when | δ(a) − δ(b) | ≤ 0.15. Without that gate, two players with identical (zero) 3-point impact read as shooting twins even when one lives at the rim and the other is a high-volume league-average bomber.

7.3Draft scoring — the app’s origin

Sixteen playoff teams are drafted eight apiece. For a won series in round r with base B = (1, 2, 4, 8) for rounds 1–4, and seeds σ:

Pseries = Br + max ( 0 , σwinner − σloser ) the seed differential pays only on an upset

and a live series projects linearly in games won:

Pseries = Pseries · games won4

with real results and user “what-if” speculation accumulated in separate ledgers so the two are never confused.

9Invariants the implementation must preserve

  1. Σc VAc = VA, to the decimal, on every surface.
  2. Baselines are season-local: a 1987 season is scored against 1987, never against today. Playoff runs use their own season’s regular-season baselines, so October and June are the same currency.
  3. Non-NBA populations get their own λ; the formula is portable, the baselines are not.
  4. A season is scored by one λ(y) from one source table; a plausibility gate (0.33 < μPTS < 0.52) refuses to write a baseline derived from a mis-parsed page.
  5. Any category the source does not carry is absent, never zero-filled — a missing measurement must not read as below-average performance.
